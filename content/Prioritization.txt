# Information about Prioritization

What is present value?

  The [present value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Present_value) of something in the future is a measurement of how much value today you would trade now in exchange for that thing in the future.

  In economics, we can compute the current value of future money via interest rates. For example, if someone has the opportunity to invest $1 today to get $1.10 next year, it means the present value of $1.10 next year is $1. Then we can divide by 1.1 to get that the present value of $1 next year is approximately $0.91.

  Similarly, $1 two years from now would be worth $1.21 so $1 at that time would be worth about $0.82 . If we want to know the present value of getting $1 every year forever (including right now), assuming a compounding rate of 1.1, this sum equals 1 + 1/1.1 + 1/1.1^2... which equals $11.

  Note that the present value of a future event is dependent on the interest rate (0.1 in the example above).

How can I estimate the cost of a project?

  Estimating the cost of a project is difficult. Here are some possible approaches:

  *   In many cases, it's not necessary to get a precise estimate, and instead it's adequate to just determine whether one task is more difficult than another.

  *   One way to estimate the difficulty of the project is to start the project and see what difficulties are found.

      * If you can identify the aspects of the project that are most unknown/risky and work on them first, this allows you to more quickly improve your estimate of the difficulty of the project

  *   These types of things tend to take more time:

      * Making large changes

      * Working in an unfamiliar system

  * Sometimes it can help to brainstorm possible solutions

    * Perhaps you will eventually think of an idea that you like better than your first idea

      * If you are interested in trying a computer game that can demonstrate this concept, see [Into the Breach](https://store.steampowered.com/app/590380/Into_the_Breach/).

Prioritizing

  What you should spend your time on depends on:
  * How valuable each of your options is
  * How difficult each of your options is
  * How hard it is to to find other ideas

  For a large task:
  * If you have an option that is highly valuable but seems very difficult, then it is often worthwhile to give it more consideration, because you may find that your estimate of the difficulty improves after further consideration.

  If you haven't found any highly valuable options, it may help to explore for more options.

  For many small, similar tasks:
  * If you would like to automate the process of keeping track of what you like and finding ideas for what to do next, you might like [ActivityRecommender](https://github.com/mathjeff/ActivityRecommender).

  For many different tasks:
  * You may be interested in writing the tasks down in a system called an issue tracker
